# Raiffy: pitch deck outline

**Event:** hackathon, Scaling & Growth track · **Audience:** Raiffeisen Bank Romania jury · **Length:** 5-minute main deck (slides 1–13) plus an appendix for Q&A (A1–A12)

**Scope:** Raiffy serves **individuals only**. Business clients are pointed to Raiffeisen's business channels (Smart Business, ERIC, an SME specialist).

**Design notes**
- Palette and type from `DESIGN.md`: near-black background with yellow `#FFD23F` and amber `#FF9A3D` used as light, ink `#1A1A1A`, Geist for text and Geist Mono for every number.
- One idea per slide. The slide title is the claim, written as a full sentence; the body proves it.
- Every figure on a slide carries a small source line. Figures marked **[bank data]** are placeholders to replace with Raiffeisen's numbers before presenting.
- The demo runs live from slide 5 to slide 7, with a recorded backup video ready.

**Title:** Romanians don't know what their money decisions really cost, and they don't trust the bank to tell them.

**On the slide**

**Speaker notes**

- The only party that knows every number is the one selling the loan.

## Appendix (Q&A backup)

### A1 · The customer experience, end to end
**Title:** From sign-in to follow-up, the customer never retypes anything, never waits without knowing why, and never meets a surprise in the price.

**Visual:** an 8-step journey
1. Sign in (Raiffeisen SSO first, marketing consent separate)
2. Ask in plain words
3. Watch it think
4. Compare routes
5. Plan summary
6. **Specialist signs** (highlighted)
7. Apply in Smart Mobile
8. Follow up

**Below it**
- A human is one tap away at every step; hardship or crisis switches off every offer.
- **Moments of truth:**
  - The teaser answer shows real reasoning before sign-in.
  - A rival's win is shown, then countered.
  - The plan matches the bank's calculator to the cent.
  - "Confirmed by Ana M."
  - No data is typed twice.

### A2 · How the recommendation is chosen
**Title:** A calculator picks the recommendation, not the AI: the lowest total cost among the loans you can get and afford.

**Rules, in order**
1. Ineligible routes are greyed with the reason (for example leasing for a car that's too old).
2. Routes that break the BNR affordability limit or fail the rate-rise check rank last, with a warning.
3. The rest rank by total amount payable: interest, every fee, mandatory insurance such as CASCO, and required packages.
4. In a near tie, the lower monthly payment wins.
5. Saving routes (deposits) appear only when the customer asks how to save up for something.

### A3 · Pricing and counter-offers
**Title:** Prices reflect the whole relationship, and negotiation follows the same rules for everyone.

- Conditional prices sit on the card next to the unconditional ones ("7.9% with salary transfer · 9.4% without"), and the cost of meeting the condition is included.
- **Evidence first:** the customer uploads the competitor's SECCI. A typed claim alone gets no counter-offer.
- **Total cost first:** many "cheaper" offers disappear once fees and insurance are counted, and Raiffy says so.
- **Inside the band:** an automatic counter-offer (fee waivers first, then a small rate cut), valid for 7 days, only for clients the bank already has a risk view of.
- **Above the band:** escalated to a specialist with the competitor offer attached. Raiffy never promises a price or hints at limits.
- Asking again never lowers the price.
- Every counter-offer is logged, which gives a win-rate KPI and shows the pricing team where the bank loses on price.

### A4 · Trust, data and privacy
**Title:** Raiffy uses only what the customer lets it see, and says where every number comes from.

- **Income is self-declared** and labelled. Raiffy never infers it from transactions and never asks for proof. It tells the customer up front which documents the bank will ask for when they apply.
- **From Smart Mobile, with the customer's switches:**
  - Existing Raiffeisen loans and cards
  - Limits and how much is used
  - Products held
  - Pre-approved offers
  - Deposits and maturities
  - Fees charged
  - Age, for eligibility
  - Identity verification status
  - Application events
- **Never used:** transaction history, salary amounts, CNP, IBAN or card numbers, location, health data.
- **To decide with Raiffeisen:** a salary-transfer flag (yes or no only), and a risk band read only by the pricing tool.
- Raiffy never opens links; the customer types the price.
- Specialists open a chat only on a trigger. Access is logged and sensitive data masked. Data is hosted in the EU.

### A5 · Deciding together, and for everyone
**Title:** Couples and families decide in one shared chat, and everyone can use Raiffy on their own terms.

- **Shared chats:**
  - Invite by link, the owner approves each person
  - Up to 5 people can ask questions
  - Every message shows who wrote it
  - Prompts are answered in parallel, one agent per prompt
  - Each person's data stays theirs unless they choose to share it
- **Accessibility:**
  - Voice in and out, with a spoken summary of every card
  - Three plain-language levels; the numbers never change between them
  - Calm mode: larger text, one card at a time, no motion
  - Full screen-reader and keyboard support (WCAG 2.1 AA; the European Accessibility Act applies to banking since June 2025)

### A6 · Customer metrics: definitions and pilot targets
**Title:** Every target is measured against a control group, so the lift is causal.

| Metric | How it's measured | Pilot target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LTV | Contribution per customer × expected lifetime, Raiffy users vs control | +10% at 12 months |
| CAC (Raiffy channel) | Layered: marginal, channel, fully loaded (A7) | 30–50% below paid digital channels |
| LTV:CAC | LTV ÷ CAC | ≥ 3:1, aiming for 5:1 |
| CAC payback | CAC ÷ monthly contribution | < 12 months |
| New-to-bank conversion | Non-client sign-ups opening an account within 90 days | 10–15% |
| Products per customer | Active products per customer vs control | +0.2 to +0.3 at 12 months |
| Churn | Customers lost per year vs control | 10–20% lower |
| Refinancing-away rate | Loans repaid early to move to a rival | 20% lower |
| 7-day / 30-day return rate | Users who come back | ≥ 15% / ≥ 30% |
| Daily / monthly active users | Daily ÷ monthly active users | 8–12% |
| Plan retention | Saved plans still active after 90 days | ≥ 60% |
| NPS · helpfulness | Surveys, thumbs up | +10 vs control · ≥ 85% |
| Plan rate | Goal conversations ending in a saved plan | ≥ 25% |
| Handoff-to-application | Applications started ÷ handoffs | 25–35%; +20% vs calculators |
| Approval rate | Raiffy applications vs other digital channels | +5 to +10 pp |
| Counter-offer win rate | Accepted ÷ counter-offers made | 30–40% |
| Containment | Information questions answered without a human | 70–80% |
| Confirmed without edits | Specialist reviews confirmed as is | ≥ 90% in month 1, ≥ 95% by month 3 |

**Anchor:** H1 2026 net profit of RON 849M across nearly 2.4M clients, about 700 lei per client per year. This is an order of magnitude, not marginal profit.

### A7 · How we count CAC
**Title:** Three layers of CAC, plus the incremental version.

- **Marginal:** tokens, specialist minutes on prospects, onboarding checks. *What does one more customer cost?*
- **Channel (the headline number):** marginal, plus the Raiffy team, hosting and marketing, allocated by the share of conversations with non-clients.
- **Fully loaded:** channel, plus the build cost amortized over 3 years and the compliance and management time spent on Raiffy.
- **People:** loaded cost × share of time spent on acquisition. Specialist minutes come straight from the console's review logs. Directors count only for the time they actually spend on Raiffy. General bank overhead is excluded, for every channel alike.
- **Not CAC:**
  - Counter-offer discounts reduce LTV.
  - Service conversations with existing clients are cost to serve.
  - Credit losses belong in LTV.
- **Incremental CAC** = channel costs ÷ (customers acquired − those the control group shows would have joined anyway).
- The cost of the next customer falls as Raiffy grows; branch CAC doesn't.

### A8 · Cost savings for Raiffeisen (12 ways)
1. Less adviser and branch time per sale: customers arrive with a compared, checked plan and a pre-filled application.
2. Fewer call-centre contacts: fee and product questions are answered in the app.
3. Lower acquisition cost: honest comparisons and shared chats bring in new customers without ad spend.
4. Fewer credit-broker commissions.
5. Retention costs less than acquisition: a counter-offer is cheaper than replacing a lost customer.
6. Less wasted underwriting: eligibility and affordability are checked before applying.
7. Lower expected credit losses: affordability first, no pressure, no offers in hardship *(needs 12+ months to prove)*.
8. Fewer complaints and less remediation: every fee shown up front *(needs 6–12 months to prove)*.
9. Cheaper compliance: pre-contract information, dated sources and audit logs are built in; automated checks replace much manual QA.
10. One catalog for every channel: a new product or price is a data change, not new code.
11. Falling human review effort: each specialist edit becomes a test.
12. Free pricing intelligence: the counter-offer log shows where and against whom the bank loses on price.

### A9 · Cost savings for customers (10 ways)
1. The cheapest route by total cost, not by monthly payment.
2. Hidden costs (CASCO, packages, insurance) seen before signing.
3. Fees avoided once Raiffy explains them.
4. Better prices through counter-offers, without haggling in a branch.
5. Cheaper refinancing of existing debt.
6. Early repayment shown clearly, including when it saves money and what it costs.
7. Idle money put to work: deposit maturity reminders.
8. The best deposit for a savings goal, when they ask.
9. Avoiding over-borrowing, and the late fees and penalties that follow.
10. Time saved: no branch visit to compare, nothing typed twice, decisions made together in one chat.

### A10 · The "money saved" method
**Title:** Only real, provable savings count.

- **What counts:**
  - A cheaper route chosen
  - Refinancing
  - Fees avoided
  - Counter-offers (reported separately, because the reference is Raiffeisen's own list price)
- **When it counts:** fee differences at signing; interest differences month by month as instalments are paid.
- **What never counts:**
  - Projections
  - Withdrawn loans
  - Stale or unconfirmed competitor data
  - The gap to the most expensive option
  - Interest earned
- The recommendation can't read the savings ledger, so the metric can't steer customers.
- Published only as rounded realized totals, with a public methodology page and compliance sign-off.

### A11 · Regulation and risk
**Title:** Built for the rules that are coming, and kept out of the credit decision.

| Topic | Raiffy's position |
| --- | --- |
| EU Consumer Credit Directive (applies 20 Nov 2026) | Affordability before offers, every cost disclosed, no pressure; whether Raiffy counts as an "advisory service" is to be confirmed with legal, and the scope line ("Showing Raiffeisen options") already supports that disclosure |
| EU AI Act (creditworthiness assessment is high-risk from 2 Dec 2027) | Raiffy shows indicative affordability only; the credit decision stays in the bank's systems; legal to confirm |
| GDPR | Separate marketing consent; trigger-only operator access, masked data, every access logged |
| Advice | No investment, tax or legal advice; refers out |
| Competitor naming | Official documents only, dated, excluded after 30 days; comparative-advertising review before launch |
| Margin erosion from counter-offers | Bands owned by the pricing team; Raiffy never invents a price |
| Specialist capacity | Review on demand or for high stakes, with daily caps and callbacks |

### A12 · Likely jury questions
| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Why show a cheaper competitor? | Customers find out anyway. Disclosing it and countering within approved bands keeps them and earns trust for the next product |
| What stops invented numbers? | The AI writes 2–3 sentences; tools compute every number; code builds every card; the evaluator rejects any untraced number |
| Is this financial advice? | It compares and explains with sourced numbers, labels plans as indicative, and refers out for investments, tax and legal |
| Does human review scale? | Only on demand or for high stakes; edits become tests, so the confirmed-without-edits rate rises |
| Does required login kill the funnel? | A visitor can ask one question and see a blurred result first; Google, Apple and email sign-in let non-clients in |
| How is this different from ERIC? | ERIC coaches business clients; Raiffy serves individuals, computes routes with real numbers, and hands off to apply |
| Why stop comparing rivals after signing? | The relationship is established, so the default shows Raiffeisen options, stated openly, with one tap to compare |
| Why self-declared income? | The bank doesn't store salary data; the bank verifies documents when the customer applies, and Raiffy says so up front |
