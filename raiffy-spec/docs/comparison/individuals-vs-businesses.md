# Individuals vs businesses

Businesses are harder, but not 10x harder everywhere: the difficulty is concentrated in five places, and most of Raiffy's core (pipeline, schema, comparison, commission transparency, safety) works the same for both. The hard parts are **messy, fast-changing data** (turnover, invoices, taxes), **cash flow instead of a salary**, **several people and roles per account**, **tax questions that must be referred out**, and **eligibility that depends on the business's history**.

Individuals ask about one life and one income. A business owner asks about a business whose numbers move every month, a personal life tangled with it (especially PFAs), and rules that changed again this year.

## Who's on the other side

| | Individual | PFA or freelancer | Micro company (SRL) | Small company |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example | Andrei, 34, salaried, wants a car | Ioana, freelance designer, irregular monthly income | Mihai, owns a car-repair shop with 4 employees | Elena, runs a food distributor with 30 employees and an accountant |
| Who decides | One person or a couple | One person, mixing personal and business money | The owner, often with an external accountant | Owner plus finance person, sometimes a second administrator |
| Money knowledge | Varies widely | Strong in their craft, often weak in finance | Practical, learned by doing | Higher; uses an accountant and reports |
| Income | Fixed salary, predictable | Irregular, seasonal, client-dependent | Variable, tied to clients and season | Larger but with long payment cycles |
| Time available | Evenings, weekends | Squeezed between client work | Very little, often on the phone at work | Some, but decisions go through more people |
| Biggest fear | Can't afford it, hidden costs | Unpredictable income, taxes, doing it wrong | Running out of cash, paying salaries | Unpaid invoices, growth outpacing cash |
| Data Raiffy can see | Account, salary, existing loans | Account, sometimes mixed with personal | Business account, if connected | Business accounts, possibly several banks |

## What each asks

Individuals mostly ask "can I afford this?"; businesses mostly ask "how do I keep cash moving?" and "am I doing this right?"

| Topic | Individual | PFA or freelancer | Micro or small company |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Buying something | "Can I afford a 2016 BMW X5?" | "Should I buy my new laptop and camera on leasing or pay cash?" | "Loan or leasing for two delivery vans?" |
| Cash | "I'm short until payday" | "Clients paid late this month, how do I cover my contributions?" | "Salaries are due on the 10th, my biggest client pays in 60 days" |
| Borrowing | "Which personal loan is cheapest?" | "Can I get a loan as a PFA with only 8 months of activity?" | "Do I qualify for a state-guaranteed loan?" |
| Saving and growth | "How do I save 20,000 lei for a car in 2 years?" | "How much should I keep aside for taxes each month?" | "Can I afford to hire two people?", "Should I open a second location?" |
| Comparing | "Is your loan better than BT's?" | "Which business account package fits my usage?" | "Is your overdraft cheaper than our current bank's?" |
| Fees | "Why was I charged 15 lei?" | "Why do I pay a package fee and transfer fees?" | "What does factoring really cost us per invoice?" |
| Rules and tax | "Is my deposit guaranteed?" | "Norm or real system?", "Do I need e-Factura?" | "Do we still qualify as a micro-enterprise this year?", "When is VAT due?" |
| Starting out | "Which account should I open?" | "Should I open a PFA or an SRL?" | "What do I need to open a company account online?" |
| Problems | "I don't recognise this payment" | "A client hasn't paid for 90 days" | "Our supplier emailed a new IBAN and wants payment today" |
| Distress | "I can't pay my rent" | "I can't pay my contributions this quarter" | "We have tax arrears and a bailiff letter" |

## What each needs more of

| Individuals need more… | Businesses need more… |
| --- | --- |
| **Reassurance and plain language**: many feel anxious or ashamed about money | **Speed and precision**: they want the answer and the next step, no hand-holding |
| **Protection**: consumer rules, hardship flows, scam warnings for vulnerable people | **Cash-flow visibility**: what comes in and goes out over the next months, and when the gap hits |
| **Affordability checks** against one income | **Eligibility clarity**: which products they can apply for given age, turnover and legal form |
| **Total-cost thinking**: running costs of a car or home | **Timing**: payment terms, invoice cycles, tax deadlines, seasonality |
| **Education**: what DAE, IRCC or a deposit guarantee means | **Referral to experts**: accountants for tax, lawyers for disputes, relationship managers for bigger deals |
| **Emotional tone** in life events (baby, divorce, job loss) | **State and EU programmes**: guarantees and grants that individuals rarely use |
| **Simple comparisons** of a few products | **Integration with their tools**: accounting software, e-Factura, payroll |
| **Privacy reassurance** | **Roles and permissions**: who can see, who can approve, multiple signatures |
| — | **Separating personal from business money**, especially for PFAs |
| — | **Up-to-date rules**: tax thresholds and programmes change yearly |

Both need the same core: honest financing-route comparisons, every commission shown, numbers from tools, a human when they want one, and answers in their language.

## How interactions differ

| Aspect | Individuals | Businesses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Session pattern | Occasional, around life events; longer, exploratory chats | Frequent, short, task-driven ("check this, then go"), often from a phone at work |
| Tone that works | Warm, reassuring, educational | Brief, numbers-first, partner-like |
| Who's in the conversation | One person | Owner, accountant, co-administrator, sometimes several over time |
| Data quality | Mostly in the bank already (salary, loans) | Spread across banks, accounting software, e-Factura and the owner's head; often estimates |
| Decision driver | Monthly payment and total cost | Cash flow, timing, eligibility, tax effect |
| Decision speed | Days to weeks | Often urgent (salaries, a supplier deadline) or slow (investment needs accountant and partners) |
| Personal vs other money | Clear | Blurred for PFAs; separate for companies but owners still think personally |
| Recurring needs | Few: a loan every few years | Many: monthly cash checks, taxes, invoices, renewals |
| Follow-up | Rarely needed | Often: "remind me before VAT is due", "watch my cash next month" |
| Mistakes cost | A worse personal deal | Missed salaries, penalties, a lost client, sometimes the business |
| Handoff to humans | Call center, branch | SME specialist, relationship manager, the business's own accountant |
| Proactive potential | Moderate (spending insights) | High (cash-gap alerts, invoice reminders, deadline nudges), with consent |

## Why business is harder

Rated relative to the individuals track (1 = no harder, 5 = much harder); these are judgment calls to prioritise work, not measurements.

| Dimension | Harder for business | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data | 5 | Real numbers live in accounting software and e-Factura, not the bank; without them every answer rests on estimates |
| Affordability logic | 5 | Cash flow over time and debt-service coverage replace one simple income-based cap |
| Rules and tax | 5 | Tax regimes, thresholds and reporting changed again for 2026; every answer touching tax needs an accountant referral |
| Eligibility | 4 | Depends on legal form, months of activity, closed financial years, turnover and state-aid history |
| People and roles | 4 | Several users with different rights; approvals and multi-signature payments |
| Product range | 3 | More routes (factoring, guarantees, grants, leasing), but they fit the same comparison engine |
| Liability | 3 | A wrong forecast can hurt a business more than a person; mitigated by scenario labels and human review |
| Personal vs business mix | 3 | Mostly PFAs; solved by asking and stating which rules apply |
| Tone and UX | 2 | Different tone and new blocks, same design system |
| Pipeline and safety | 1 | Same gate, router, orchestrator, evaluator and schema |
| Language and accessibility | 1 | Identical |

So business is much harder in **data, affordability, rules and eligibility**, moderately harder in roles and products, and about the same everywhere else. The "10x" feeling comes from the first four, which is where effort should go.

## How to make the business track manageable

Attack the four hardest dimensions directly, and narrow the scope until each is solved.

| Hard part | Practical solution |
| --- | --- |
| Data | Start every business session with a short `businessProfile` (legal form, months active, turnover band, VAT status, employees) and reuse it; connect bank data first, accounting and e-Factura later, with consent; label every estimate as one |
| Affordability | One deterministic cash-flow tool used by every pattern; show scenarios ("if your client pays 30 days late") instead of a single prediction |
| Rules and tax | Explain, never advise: dated config for every threshold, an owner who reviews it, and an accountant referral with the exact questions to ask |
| Eligibility | An `eligibilityCheck` tool that runs before any comparison, so owners never see products they can't get, with the reason shown for each one excluded |
| People and roles | Answers follow the user's rights from business banking; approvals always happen in Smart Business, not in chat |
| Personal vs business | The router asks once, remembers the answer, and every answer states which rules apply |

**Narrow the scope in stages**

1. **Start with PFAs and liberal professions.** They're closest to individuals (one person, simpler data), so most of the individuals track carries over.
2. **Then three high-value business patterns**: cash-flow gap, equipment (loan vs leasing vs cash), and funding programmes.
3. **Then micro companies**, adding roles, e-Factura data and factoring.
4. **Small companies last**, once data integrations and human review are in place.

**Lean on what businesses value most**

- Proactive help with consent: cash-gap alerts, tax-deadline reminders, unpaid-invoice nudges. This is where the business track can be more useful than the individuals one.
- A fast path to a human: SME specialists and relationship managers for anything above a set amount or complexity.
- Accountant-friendly outputs: exportable summaries an owner can forward to their accountant in one tap.

Done this way, the business track is roughly twice the work of the individuals track, not ten times, because the core is shared and the hard parts are isolated in a few tools, a profile, and dated config.
